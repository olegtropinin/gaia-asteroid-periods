Dear Dr. Lintott,

Thank you for the clear comments. I have rewritten the Note so that the method can be reproduced from the text alone. The changes are:

1. Model ("which terms can vary, what is the offset, what is a second-order Fourier series in rotational phase"). The model is now written as an equation (Eq. 1): reduced magnitude = c_s(i) + beta*alpha_i + sum over k=1,2 of [a_k cos(2 pi k f t_i) + b_k sin(2 pi k f t_i)]. All coefficients are free and linear: one magnitude offset per observing season (absorbing aspect changes between seasons), one phase-angle slope, and four Fourier amplitudes, solved by weighted least squares at each trial frequency. The text states that no phase-angle-bisector correction is applied, so the periods are synodic.

2. Data steps now defined exactly: the per-transit averaging (unweighted means of epoch and g_mag over the CCD observations), the uncertainty formula, the Horizons query (observer 500@-139479), the light-time correction, the definition of a season (gaps longer than 60 d), and the outlier cut (5 x 1.4826 x MAD of the residuals in magnitudes, applied twice).

3. Search and decision rules: the frequency grid (0.24-12 cycles per day, step 0.1/T), the period-doubling rule (adopt 2P1 when chi2(f1/2) < 1.5 chi2(f1)), how the factor 1.5 was chosen (two-fold cross-validation, factors 1.00-3.00), the definition of agreement (within 3%), the confidence score and its exclusion windows, and the threshold rule (S >= 0.1604, the last point at which the cumulative agreement on the ranked validation set is still >= 80%).

4. "The 34 candidates." A new section explains step by step how they were selected: 165 confident target-set results; minus 97 solved by Durech & Hanus (2023), 25 published elsewhere and 35277 (whose only published period is an astrometric wobble period); minus 11857, found in Durech et al. (2022); minus seven whose period a second, full-DR3 analysis contradicts. Table 1 (which replaces the figure) lists all 34 with period, amplitude, number of transits and their tier in the second analysis (18 A, 12 B, 4 below).

5. Validation figures. The abstract now quotes the out-of-sample agreement, 96 of 122 (79%), rather than a total that included the in-sample validation set. While revising I found and corrected a counting error: one comparison (Liberato et al. 2024) is a wobble period, not a rotation period, so the literature check is 15 of 25 (not 16 of 25) and the period bins now add up to the stated totals.

The Note is about 950 words with one table. Code and data are archived on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23624115).
