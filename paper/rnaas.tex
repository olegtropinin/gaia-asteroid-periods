\title{Candidate Rotation Periods for 42 Main-belt Asteroids from Gaia DR3 Sparse Photometry}

\author{Oleg Tropinin}
\affiliation{Independent researcher, Kazakhstan}

\begin{abstract}
We searched Gaia DR3 sparse photometry of the 6{,}000 asteroids that Gaia observed most often for
rotation periods. A periodogram with season offsets, a linear phase term and a second-order Fourier
series was calibrated on asteroids with known periods. On 337 confident results that can be checked
against published periods, 80\% agree within 3\%. Accuracy is 81--89\% for periods below 50\,h and
36\% above it, where the method usually returns half of the true period. We report 42 asteroids that
had no rotation period in LCDB, JPL SBDB, DAMIT, about 180 VizieR tables, or the Minor Planet Bulletin
2023--2026, and give their candidate periods with a reliability flag as targets for confirmation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data and method}

We took the 6{,}000 numbered asteroids with the most Gaia DR3 Solar-System-object observations
\citep{Tanga2023}. They were split into a validation set (300 asteroids with a period in JPL SBDB, which
follows the October 2023 revision of the LCDB; \citealt{Warner2009}) and a target set (298 without one).
Gaia photometry was averaged per transit, with a 0.03\,mag error floor. For every transit, JPL Horizons
gave the heliocentric distance $r$, the distance $\Delta$ from Gaia and the phase angle $\alpha$, with Gaia
as the observer. Times were corrected for light time.

The reduced magnitude $G-5\log_{10}(r\Delta)$ was fit by weighted least squares with one offset per
observing season, a linear phase term and a second-order Fourier series in rotational phase. We scanned
2--100\,h on a frequency grid with a step of $0.1/T$, where $T$ is the time span. Because most asteroid
lightcurves are double-peaked, we adopted the doubled period whenever doubling raised $\chi^2$ by less than
a factor of 1.5. This factor was chosen by two-fold cross-validation. The confidence score combines the
$\chi^2$ gain over a no-rotation model with the separation of the best minimum from the next one outside
the $\times1$, $\times2$ and $\times\frac12$ aliases. The threshold was set where precision on the
validation set reaches 80\%.

\section{Validation}

The cross-validated recovery rate on the validation set is 67.7\%. Above the threshold, we compared every
result whose period is known (Figure~\ref{fig:1}):
\begin{itemize}
\item the validation set (172 of 214 correct);
\item target-set asteroids that \citet{Durech2023} solved from the same Gaia data (81 of 97);
\item 26 of our initial candidates that later appeared in \citet{Cellino2024}, \citet{MacLennan2026},
\citet{McNeill2023} or \citet{Liberato2024} (17 of 26).
\end{itemize}
In total, 269 of 337 (80\%) agree.

There are two failure modes. Above 50\,h the method usually returns half of the true period (10 of 28
correct). Slow rotators with periods beyond 100\,h, such as 214, 818 and about 4{,}600\,h, receive
spurious short periods.

\section{Results}

The 42 candidates are all main-belt asteroids. They were absent from:
\begin{itemize}
\item JPL SBDB and LCDB~V4.0;
\item \citet{Durech2023};
\item DAMIT \citep{Durech2010};
\item about 180 VizieR tables (5.1 million rows) that hold asteroid rotation periods;
\item the full text of the Minor Planet Bulletin, volumes 50--53.
\end{itemize}

For the 34 candidates below 50\,h we expect about 83\% to be correct. For the 8 above 50\,h, the true period
is probably twice the value or longer. Periods, alias periods, amplitudes, amounts of data and flags are
available at \url{https://github.com/olegtropinin/gaia-asteroid-periods} and archived on Zenodo
\citep{Tropinin2026}, together with the code and the validation tables. We offer the list as targets for dense-lightcurve confirmation.
